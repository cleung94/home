Finding Direction Again: My Next Step Toward Project Management

A personal reflection on stepping away from blogging, navigating a difficult job search, rebuilding my confidence, and choosing to invest in the career path I want to pursue next.

August 2026 6 min read

It has been a little while since I last sat down and wrote something for this blog. The absence was not because I stopped learning, working on myself, or thinking about what I wanted to do next. In many ways, the opposite was true. I was spending so much energy trying to figure out my next professional chapter that I needed to step away from documenting it for a while.

Over the past several months, a large part of my time has been dedicated to finding my next opportunity. I have applied to positions, spoken with recruiters, met hiring managers, prepared for interviews, researched companies, and made it through multiple stages of the interview process. There have been opportunities where I reached some of the final conversations and genuinely felt that I might be approaching the end of the search.

Then I would hear something I was not expecting.

Sometimes the answer was that I was overqualified. Other times, I was told that my background might be better suited for another position that could become available in the future.

When Progress Does Not Feel Like Progress

Those conversations have been complicated emotionally because they are not necessarily negative feedback. In fact, being told that a company sees value in my experience or wants to consider me for something else can be encouraging. At the same time, when you have been searching for work for months, preparing for each opportunity, and repeatedly getting close without receiving an offer, it can become mentally exhausting.

Job searching has a strange way of turning your professional experience into something you constantly reevaluate. You start looking at your resume differently. You replay interview answers in your head. You wonder whether you should have explained something another way, pursued a slightly different role, or emphasized another part of your experience.

There were moments where I questioned whether I was moving in the right direction at all.

That was probably the biggest reason I stopped blogging for a while. Writing usually gives me an opportunity to organize my thoughts, but I realized I needed some time where I was not constantly thinking about applications, interviews, certifications, resumes, or what my next professional move needed to be.

Giving Myself Time Away From the Search

I decided to spend more time focusing on something that is easy to overlook when career uncertainty takes up so much mental space: my family.

I spent more time with my parents and my sister. I gave myself permission to enjoy those moments without feeling like every free hour needed to become another job application, another resume revision, or another interview preparation session.

That time helped more than I expected.

It reminded me that my career is an important part of my life, but it is not the only measurement of whether I am moving forward. Sometimes progress also means recognizing when you need to reset, reconnect with people who matter to you, and give yourself enough space to regain the confidence that can slowly disappear during a long job search.

Stepping away for a little while did not mean giving up on my career. It gave me the space to remember what I actually wanted from it.

Looking at My Experience Differently

One thing that became clearer to me during this period was how much of my previous work has already involved project management, even when project manager was not necessarily my official title.

Throughout my career in physical security systems and data analytics, I have worked across technical teams, project managers, vendors, engineers, leadership, and other stakeholders. I have helped coordinate deployments, manage timelines, build standardized workflows, identify project risks, improve quality assurance processes, analyze operational performance, communicate project status, and create tools that made work more consistent and efficient.

One of the projects I am especially proud of involved helping redesign an audit and validation process with an eight-person team. Through improvements to the workflow, we reduced a process that previously took approximately nine months to about three months.

At the time, I primarily thought about accomplishments like that through a technical or analytical lens. Now I look at them differently. There was planning involved. There were stakeholders. There were deadlines, risks, dependencies, process improvements, communication challenges, and measurable outcomes.

Those are project management problems.

Choosing Project Management Intentionally

That realization helped me make an important decision about where I want to invest my time next.

I recently began working toward the Google Project Management Professional Certificate through Coursera. I did not start the program simply because I wanted another certification to place on my resume. I started it because project management represents the direction I genuinely want my career to move toward.

The program is giving me an opportunity to take skills I have developed through experience and connect them to a more formal project management framework. Concepts such as stakeholder management, project lifecycles, risk management, communication planning, scheduling, Agile methodologies, documentation, and team coordination are areas I have encountered throughout my career in different forms.

Now I am learning how those pieces fit together within project management as a discipline.

I am not trying to leave my technical background behind. I want to use it as the foundation for becoming a stronger project manager.

Why Project Management Makes Sense for Me

What attracts me to project management is that it sits between the technical work and the people responsible for making that work successful.

I enjoy solving technical problems, working with data, and understanding how systems operate. But throughout my career, I have also found myself naturally interested in what happens around the technical work: How can the process be improved? Who needs to be involved? What is preventing the team from moving forward? Are expectations clearly defined? How do we know whether a project is healthy? What information does leadership need to make a decision?

Those questions have consistently been part of how I approach my work.

My goal is to eventually work in a project or technical project management role where I can combine that experience. I want to understand the technology well enough to communicate effectively with technical teams while also being able to manage priorities, stakeholders, timelines, risks, and business objectives.

The Google PM Certificate Is the First Step

Right now, my focus is completing the Google Project Management Certificate and using the coursework to strengthen areas where experience alone may not have given me formal terminology or structure.

I am approaching the program the same way I approach most things I learn: I do not just want to complete the lessons. I want to connect them back to situations I have actually experienced.

When a course discusses stakeholders, I think about the different teams I supported during global physical security projects. When it discusses project schedules, I think about commissioning timelines and deployment readiness. When it discusses risk, I think about identifying issues that could delay validation or prevent a system from being successfully handed over. When it discusses process improvement, I think about the workflows and automation tools I helped build.

Connecting the concepts to real experiences makes the material much more meaningful to me.

After Google, My Goal Is the PMP

Completing the Google certificate is not where I want this path to end.

After finishing the program, my longer-term goal is to pursue the Project Management Professional, or PMP, certification. I see that as another important milestone in transitioning the project management experience I have accumulated throughout my career into a more deliberate professional direction.

There is still a lot I want to learn before getting there. I want to continue strengthening my understanding of formal project management methodology, Agile environments, budgeting, project scheduling, resource planning, risk management, and stakeholder communication.

More importantly, I want to become the kind of project manager who understands that successful projects are not only about completing tasks on a schedule. They are about creating clarity for teams, removing obstacles, communicating honestly, making informed decisions, and helping people work toward a shared outcome.

Redefining What This Period Means

I could look back at the past several months entirely through the lens of not having found my next position yet. For a while, that was probably how I was measuring everything.

I am trying not to look at it that way anymore.

The interviews have helped me become better at explaining my experience. Conversations with hiring managers have shown me where companies see value in my background. Being considered for other positions has encouraged me to think more broadly about where my skills fit. Even the disappointing outcomes have forced me to think carefully about the type of work I actually want to pursue instead of simply searching for the next available role.

Most importantly, this period gave me enough time to realize that project management is not just something I am considering because of the job market. It is something I can see myself continuing to develop for years.

Not every period of growth looks like moving forward quickly. Sometimes growth is stopping long enough to figure out which direction you actually want to move.

Moving Forward Again

I am still searching for the right opportunity, and I know there may be more interviews, more applications, and probably more uncertainty ahead.

The difference now is that I feel like I have a clearer direction.

I want to keep building from the technical and analytical experience that has shaped my career while developing the project management skills that can help me lead larger initiatives in the future. The Google Project Management Certificate is one step in that process. Pursuing the PMP will be another.

And after taking some time away, I also want to begin writing here again.

This blog has always been a place where I can document what I am learning, what I am building, and how my career continues to evolve. I think it is equally important to document periods like this one—the moments where the path is less straightforward, confidence gets tested, and progress looks different than expected.

I may not know exactly what position comes next yet, but I have a much better understanding of the professional I want to become.

Key Takeaways

  • A long job search can be mentally exhausting, especially when opportunities repeatedly reach the final stages without becoming an offer.
  • Taking time away from career pressure helped me reconnect with my family and rebuild my confidence.
  • My previous technical and analytical work included many project management responsibilities, even without the formal title.
  • I have started the Google Project Management Professional Certificate to build a stronger formal foundation in project management.
  • After completing the Google program, my longer-term goal is to pursue the PMP certification.
  • I want to combine my technical background, data experience, and project leadership skills as I move toward project and technical project management roles.
  • Sometimes progress is not about moving faster. It is about gaining enough clarity to move in the right direction.
